# BYOS messaging platform: "2027 is the year of custom software"

Working document. Audience: startups and everyone above them. Tone: aggressive about the cost, blunt about the limits.
Every number here comes from our own analysis of 44 tools' public pages (automated, imperfect) unless stated. Section 9 lists which claims are verified and which are not.

---

## 1. The position in one paragraph

Software was priced for the vendor's economics: build once, sell to thousands, stack every feature any customer might want, charge per seat. When building the five features you need stops costing months, the hundred-feature product stops being a deal. You pay for all of it, learn all of it, and work around the part that doesn't fit.

**Our enemy is bloat priced per seat, not "SaaS".** We are not announcing the death of anything.

**Our wedge is honesty.** Every "build your own" tool says it can build anything. The market has noticed: the loudest complaint about AI builders is the demo that breaks the moment you use it. We are the one that reads the tool you pay for, sorts every feature into *we build it / we build it but you bring the infrastructure / simplified / we can't*, and says so before you spend anything. Nobody else shows the fine print first.

### Positioning statement
For teams paying per seat for tools they use a slice of, WyberAi builds your own version of just the features you pick, and tells you up front what needs an outside service and what can't be built at all.

### The one-liner
**Paste the tool you pay for. Keep the features you use. We'll tell you straight what we can't build.**

### The campaign line (the 2027 layer)
**2027: stop renting software you use 5% of.**
Use it as a banner and a theme. The working product line stays concrete. See section 9 on why "5%" and "100 features" need care.

## 2. Why this lands now (and where the market is lying)

- The "SaaSpocalypse" story is loud. Bolt publishes "The 4 Horsemen of the SaaSpocalypse", and most pieces on the topic come from companies that sell AI building. Readers can smell that.
- The sober reading is that **thin, horizontal, per-seat tools are the exposed ones, and platforms with a network, compliance or infrastructure moat survive.** Bolt's own piece says to keep buying systems of record. The UK NCSC makes the same split and cites a startup whose renewal quote doubled when growth pushed it into a higher tier, so an engineer rebuilt the core in hours.
- There is **no hard data on how many companies actually cancelled.** Anyone quoting a cancellation number is guessing.

**Our opening:** be the voice that says the true thing the hype won't. *"SaaS isn't dead. Here are the parts of your tools that don't need to be rented."* That position also makes our own Slack, Zoom and Teams verdict ("we can't replace all of it") feel consistent instead of like a limitation.

Sources: [Bolt on the SaaSpocalypse](https://bolt.new/blog/is-saas-dying), [UK NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/vibe-check-ai-may-replace-saas-but-not-for-a-while), [market summary](https://thegtmnewsletter.substack.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-revaluation-ai-era-value-shift).

## 3. Audiences

### A. Startups (founders, ops leads, first engineers)
- **Pain:** seat cost grows with every hire. Eight tools that don't talk to each other. Pricing tiers that jump when they grow.
- **What they want to hear:** I can stop negotiating with software. It will fit how we work.
- **Proof they need:** speed to a first version, a price before they start, and that it won't fall over.

### B. Above startups (mid-market, enterprise teams: ops, finance, IT, product)
- **Pain:** shelfware, seat audits, renewals with a built-in increase, vendor roadmaps that aren't theirs, security reviews of every new tool.
- **What they want to hear:** I'm not trading a vendor for a liability.
- **Proof they need:** ownership and export of the code, a built-in security scan, and an honest account of what is not covered. Our fit check is the credibility device here. Do not claim certifications, SSO or SLAs for generated apps. The fit check itself tells them SSO needs an identity provider.

## 4. Message architecture

Three pillars. Each has one proof that exists in the product today.

| Pillar | The claim | Proof in the product |
|---|---|---|
| **Pick** | Pay for the slice you use, not the whole tool. | Paste a URL, get the tool's features grouped, tick what you use. 44 tools are instant. |
| **Honest** | We tell you what we can't build, before you spend. | Every feature is tagged. Slack, Zoom and HubSpot get "not a one-to-one replacement". The quote shows the outside services you'd need (for example Amazon SES for bulk email). |
| **Own** | It's your code and your data, with no seats. | Code export to GitHub, working sign-in with sample data, a database that is yours (your own Supabase account, never ours), a built-in security scan, an estimated price before you start. |

### Vocabulary (use these words)
- "the tool you pay for" (not "legacy SaaS")
- "features you use" / "the slice"
- "we write the software; you rent only what has to be rented"
- "we can't build this" (say it plainly)
- "your own version"

### Never say
- "revolutionary", "supercharge", "seamless", "unlock", "10x", "game-changing", "AI-powered"
- "SaaS is dead" (hype, and false by our own verdict on Teams and Slack)
- any savings, earnings or time-saved figure we haven't measured
- "free", "free build", discounts (credit policy is fixed)
- anything about team size, location or the founder in business copy (company voice only)
- any apology or admission of fault in customer messages
- a competitor's name in paid ad copy (trademark). Use "your CRM", "your scheduling tool". Their names are fine inside the product and on the replace pages, where the user typed them.

## 6. The honesty campaign (the part nobody else can copy)

The fit check turns into content because it's real data. A competitor would have to build the same analysis first.

### 6.1 The report: "We read the public pages of 44 tools"
Findings from our own analysis (1,073 features across 44 tools). Publish with the method and the caveat that this is automated.
- **52%** of features we can build as-is.
- **31%** we can build, but they only work on an outside service you pay for. The most common: AI models (114 features), notification email (72), payments (34), single sign-on (21), SMS and WhatsApp (20), calendar sync (20).
- **9%** only a simplified version is realistic.
- **8%** we can't build.
- The 8% is the interesting part: marketplaces, certifications, networks, native apps.

Headline options: *"83% of what you rent is buildable. Here is the 8% that isn't."* or *"What the 44 most-replaced tools don't want you to know you can build."* (second one is slop-adjacent, prefer the first.)
**Hold until a person has read every verdict (section 9).** The test builds passed (a 12-feature and two 6-feature Pipedrive builds, local and live), so the build claim is now backed. The 83% buildable figure is still only as good as the model-plus-rules classification behind it.

### 6.2 Series: "Things we can't build"
One post per platform, weekly. Each is a page we already have.
1. Slack: the network and the 2,600 apps.
2. Zoom: the video and phone network.
3. Payroll (Gusto/BambooHR): tax filing.
4. Mailchimp: deliverability reputation.
5. Any tool where compliance is the product.
Ending of each: *what we can build instead*, with the replace-page link.

### 6.3 "When you should NOT replace it" (shareable checklist)
- It's a system of record with compliance behind it: payroll, ledgers, health records.
- Its value is the other side of a network: marketplaces, shared channels.
- It runs infrastructure at scale: telephony, video, email deliverability.
- You can't staff the uptime if it breaks.
This list is a trust asset. Put it on `/byos` below the fold.

## 8. Outbound (passes the "swap the name" test)

The rule: **open with the artifact, not with "I noticed".** The artifact is the replace page for the tool they pay for, which is specific to them and cannot be sent to anyone else.

### 8.1 Email: startup founder or ops lead
**Subject:** {Tool}: its {N} features, sorted by what's rebuildable

> Hi {first},
>
> We read {Tool}'s public pages and listed the {N} features it advertises, then marked each one: buildable as-is, buildable on an outside service (like Amazon SES for bulk email), simplified, or not buildable. {X} are straightforward and {Y} we can't build.
>
> The list is here: wyberai.com/byos/{slug}
>
> If your team uses six or seven of them, WyberAi can build just those and you own the result. If you rely on the parts we can't build, the page says so, and you should probably keep paying for it.
>
> Which features does your team actually open?

### 8.2 Email: above startups (ops, finance, IT)
**Subject:** What a {Tool} replacement would and wouldn't cover

> Hi {first},
>
> This is the honest version of a "build vs. buy" page for {Tool}: every feature, what we can build, what needs a provider you'd contract with directly (and who), and what we won't attempt. Code is exportable to GitHub and there's a security scan on the result.
>
> wyberai.com/byos/{slug}
>
> If you're mapping which tools to renew this year, it takes five minutes to see whether a narrower version would do.

### 8.3 Follow-up (one, short)
> {first}, one number from the {Tool} page: {X} of its {N} features need nothing outside the app we'd build. Happy to walk through which ones, or leave the page with you.

### Rules
- Fill `{N}`, `{X}`, `{Y}` from the actual replace page for that tool. Never round up.
- Send only to addresses on the approved test/owned domains until the allowlist rule is lifted.
- One follow-up. No fake "just checking in" sequences.

| Your tools run on your own accounts and keys | **Verified** | Server code ships as `api/` functions that read the owner's own settings. A live deploy returned configured: true for SES, Twilio and the AI key. We never pass their calls through our accounts. |

| Built-in security scan | Exists in code | Same. |
| Real sign-in and real database | **Disproved by the Nov 4 test build** | The Pipedrive test build shipped localStorage auth with demo accounts and seeded data, no Supabase, despite the prompt asking for a real database. Founder decisions (Nov 4): we do not host customer databases, and every connected tool runs on the customer's own account and API key. Our job is to write the code and put it on their domain once they have entered their settings. |

## 10. Objections, answered straight

| They say | We say |
|---|---|
| "AI-built software is insecure." | Fair worry, and the research agrees. Ours comes with a built-in security scan and your own database; you can export the code and have anyone review it. It's your decision whether to trust it. |
| "We'd have to maintain it." | Yes. You own it, and changes cost credits. For a tool you use six features of, that's often less than the seat bill. Not always, and the quote tells you the building cost. |
| "What about SSO, audit logs, compliance?" | Fit check says it: SSO needs an identity provider, certifications can't be built by us. If you need those, keep the vendor. |
| "Why not just keep paying?" | Often you should. Systems of record, networks, anything where compliance is the product. We tell you which. |
| "Integrations?" | Live for a defined list; the rest get a labelled placeholder. Nothing pretends to work. |
| "Is this just a demo?" | That's the failure everyone complains about. The quote lists exactly what it needs and nothing is faked. (Re-verify after the test build.) |

## 12. Decisions for the founder
1. **Which hero?** "100 features / 5" (your line, punchy, not our data) vs. a line we can defend ("the whole tool / a slice").
2. **Lead with honesty or lead with cost?** My recommendation: cost wins the click, honesty wins the signup. Use cost in ads, honesty on the page.
3. **Do we publish the 44-tool report?** Strong asset, but only after the test build proves the 83% isn't a promise we can't keep.
4. **Platforms:** keep "not a one-to-one replacement" on Teams-class products. It's the line that makes everything else believable.
